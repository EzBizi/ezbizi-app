Scheduling · Locations, made easier to read

Jose, 2026-10-07, with a screenshot of Scheduling › Locations: "make this page look better". Today's page works but reads poorly. Every row repeats its company in front of the branch ("A-1 Chimney · A1 Chimney - Denver"). The phone floats far to the right of a sparse row. The state headings are tiny two-letter codes. Twenty-six state chips take two rows. There are two search boxes that look the same but do different things. And the selected branch shows only as blue text, beside a sparse detail card.

Data: nothing new. Every option keeps every feature: the closest branch to a customer's address (with who serves the ZIP), the whole directory filtered by name or state, and each branch's address, phone, email, area, what it serves, the note for new dispatchers, Check availability, Directions, Copy. In all of them: a row names the branch once with its company as a small label; states read in full ("Connecticut · 8"); the selected row is shaded; the phone sits in its own column; and the detail panel groups each fact with its action: call or copy the number, directions or copy the address.

1The same two panes, tidied: one clear lookup, a tight directory, a detail card that acts Recommended

The address lookup becomes the page's headline: Who serves this address?, a wider box with its button spelled out. The directory below is a tight list: branch name in bold, its company and city small under it, the area and phone in their own columns, and states as full-name section headings. A State dropdown replaces the 26 chips, beside a name filter labelled for what it does. The selected branch is shaded, and its card on the right groups each fact with its action. Why: it is still the page people know, but it reads at a glance, and a dispatcher's two jobs (who serves this address, who do I call) each have one obvious place.
