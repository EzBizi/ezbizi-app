Communication · Alerts

From the Deployment Lead, for Jose: send an alert to chosen people, and it pops up on their screen. Jose, verbatim: "This sort of alert notifications should be a pop up on the screen, that they can dismiss". Someone whose role allows it writes the alert in a new Alerts tab under Communication and picks the people. Each person sees it pop up until they dismiss it, and the sender can see who has.

Who builds what: Notifications builds the server: send an alert, list the sent ones with who has dismissed each, list a person's open alerts, and dismiss one. Phone owns the Communication tab. UI/UX builds the screens. In every option: an alert arrives within about a minute, because the app checks with the bell, once a minute and whenever it comes back to the front. If EzBizi is closed, the alert pops up the next time they open it. It stays until they press Dismiss, and dismissing it on one device clears it on all of them. A dismissed alert stays in the bell's list. Sending needs a permission (Communication › Alerts: send), set in Roles & permissions.

1A card in the top corner that stays until dismissed; write and track on one screen Recommended

What they see: the alert pops up as a card in the top-right corner, over whatever they are doing. It stays there until they press Dismiss. Several stack up, newest first; past three, a "+2 more" tag. Sending: the Alerts tab, with the alert being written on the left (people as chips, a message, Send) and the sent ones on the right, each showing how many people have dismissed it. Click one to see who. Why: nobody can miss it, but it does not stop a dispatcher mid-call. It also follows the app's rule against layers that block the screen.

2In the middle of the screen, over a dimmed app; written in a side panel

What they see: the alert pops up in the middle of the screen and dims everything behind it. Nothing else can be clicked until they press Dismiss. If there are several, they come one after another. Sending: the Alerts tab is the list of sent alerts, and New alert opens the side panel to write one. Why: it is the hardest to miss, but it stops whatever they were doing, including a call or a half-typed job. It would also be the app's first layer that blocks the screen, which the app's rules otherwise avoid.

3A strip across the top of the page; write and track in one column

What they see: an amber strip across the top of whatever page they are on, with the message and Dismiss. If there are several, it says "1 of 3" with arrows. Sending: one column, with the alert being written at the top and the sent ones underneath. Why: it never covers anything, but it is the easiest of the three to read past. A strip is also how the app shows that someone is remotely controlling the computer, so an alert could be taken for that kind of standing notice.
