Jose, 2026-09-22: “I want to revamp the Integrations Screen, i’d like to have cards instead each with their own screen for connections, etc, something like the second image as an example but with our style of course. Remember to take into account org - company - franchise ownership, etc.”

What is wrong with it today is not that it is crowded. A1-Group has fifty-three Google Ads accounts and a column of rows that each read “A-1 Chimney · Thumbtack · this company”. The screen scrolls about four pages and answers nothing.

⚠ AND THE CAUSE IS SMALLER THAN THE SYMPTOM, WHICH CHANGES WHAT THESE OPTIONS COST. scopeWord at integrations_screen.dart:434 maps a scope type to a phrase — organization / “this company” / “this branch”. The identity never reaches the client at all: SocialConnectionsController:92 already sends scope_id, and _Connection.fromJson drops it. So “grouped by named company” needs one field in a parser and no server change. Same for last_reached_at, also sent and also dropped.

⚠ THE STATUS LINE IS THE REAL DECISION HERE, not the card shape. Nobody opens this screen to be told things are connected — they open it because something stopped working. status, status_reason and last_reached_at are all on the wire. A card reading “53 connected” answers a question nobody asked and hides the one row out of fifty-three that matters. Every option below leads with what is wrong when anything is, and falls back to the plain count when nothing is. Use the state switcher — that is what it is for.

Option 3 is the odd one and it is deliberate. The screen’s own docblock says it exists because “what is this company connected to” was not a question anybody could answer. Cards-by-provider answer “who uses Google Ads”. Those are different questions and only one of them is the one written down, so one option groups by place instead and lets Jose see both readings.

What I could not settle and Settings owns: the ownership rules — which scopes may hold which provider, and what a branch-level reader is allowed to see of a company-level connection. Every option draws state D as a guess at that (a branch reader sees only their own). If the rule is different the drawing changes, not the layout.
Everything that works today moves rather than changes — the rename dialog, the Airtable and Workiz connect forms, their two permission gates, and the “connect it where the module owns it” pointer. None of these options alter what connecting does; they alter where it is found.
