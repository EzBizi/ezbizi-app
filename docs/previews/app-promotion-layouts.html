Letting people know there's an app

Jose, 2026-10-08: "Feel like we need a way to promote the app as well, so people who are using the web or desktop know there's an app, something seamless, any ideas." The phone apps are on the App Store and Google Play, and the computer apps are at ezbizi.com/download. The person who most needs telling is someone who has never signed in on a phone. The app already knows that, because every sign-in records its device.

In every option: the QR code opens one link, ezbizi.com/app, which sends an iPhone to the App Store, an Android to Google Play and a computer to the download page. Nobody has to pick. A "No thanks" is remembered for that person, on every device. Nobody who already uses the phone app is ever asked. Small server asks: tell the phone app's sign-ins apart from a phone browser's (the app sends its own header), and the /app link on the website. Text me the link (in 1) sends the store link to the mobile number on the person's own profile, from the company's EzBizi number.

1Once, on the Dashboard, for people with no phone app; after that, "Get the apps" in the account menu Recommended

The nudge: someone who has never signed in on the phone app sees one card at the top of their Dashboard. It has a QR code to scan, "Text me the link", and "No thanks", which hides it for good. It never comes back once they install. Always there: Get the apps in the menu under their name opens the side panel: the phone app's QR and store buttons, and on a browser, the computer app for this machine. Why: it asks the right people once, in the place they look first, and asks nobody twice.

3A strip across the top after signing in on a browser

On the web, right after signing in: "EzBizi is also an app, on your phone and on Windows or Mac", with Get the apps and ✕. It comes back after a month if they dismiss it and still have no app. Why: it reaches every browser user, including people who would install the computer app. But it is the most like an ad, and a strip at the top is also how the app warns that the computer is being remotely controlled.
