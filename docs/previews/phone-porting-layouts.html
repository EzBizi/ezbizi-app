Read from porting_pane.dart “Move a number here from another carrier. Submitting and tracking happens here.” A port-in takes numbers — “One per line. They must all be on the same losing carrier.” — and then has a status and sometimes a reason. Port-out is not done here at all: “Port-out is started by the carrier you are moving TO, not here.” Empty state kept: “No ports yet. Numbers you already own elsewhere can be moved here.” This is one of the four panes with no retry today; under the corrected rule it gains one in the head, because its error is a failed load.
The finding: the most consequential sentence on the screen is a form hint “The losing carrier decides when this completes. Do not cancel [service with them].” Cancelling with the old carrier kills the port and can lose the number permanently. It is the one irreversible mistake available anywhere in this pane, and it is currently delivered as grey helper text under a text field — the same weight as “one per line”. Somebody who submits a port and then tidies up their old account is doing the obvious thing, and nothing on this screen is shaped to stop them. Every layout promotes it to a bordered block that survives after submission, because the danger is not while you are filling the form — it is during the days afterwards, when the form is gone and the port is just a row with a status.
Two things that are true and unusual, and the design leans into both 1 · No completion date is knowable. “The losing carrier decides when this completes.” So the tracker deliberately has no ETA and no percentage — it names the stage and says who is holding it. A progress bar implying a schedule would be the same dishonesty as attestation drawing a 0% bar or Scheduling saying “remaining”, and this is the third pane where the honest answer is to name the limit rather than estimate around it. 2 · Port-out is a section whose entire content is “not here”. That is worth keeping and worth drawing as a redirection rather than as an empty feature — somebody looking for it should find the answer, not an absence. All three layouts keep it; they differ in how much room it gets.
