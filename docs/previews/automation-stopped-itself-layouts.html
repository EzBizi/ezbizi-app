An automation stopped itself — pick one desktop and one mobile

Automations asked for these. Today noteFailure() counts consecutive failures, disables the rule, and tells nobody — the card is the only place it appears, and it is a card you would open only if you already suspected. So a thing somebody relies on stops, and the way they find out is that a customer did not get something.

The spine of all six: a notification is a MOMENT and a stopped automation is a CONDITION. A bell row is read once and goes away; the rule is still off. Anything that only announces is wrong for something that persists until somebody acts — so the question each option answers differently is what remains on screen for as long as the rule is off, and whether the moment is worth announcing at all.

MEASURED, and it decides what the words may claim. Automations: automation_runs keeps the thrown text and a status per run, but consecutive_failures is a live counter, not a history. So “it failed 5 times in a row” is provable at the moment of disabling and cannot be reconstructed afterwards. A sentence composed later may honestly say when it stopped and what the last error was, and may not mention the run of failures — unless it was written at disable time and stored. Every option below says only what its own moment can support.

AND THE AUDIENCE QUESTION IS NOT INDEPENDENT OF THIS CHOICE, which is worth knowing while it sits with Jose. A bell notification is per person and cannot be written until somebody decides who — owner, franchise admins, or whoever last edited the rule — and audienceFor(null) falling through to every active user makes a wrong answer worse than the gap. A glance and a badge are per SCOPE and need no addressee at all. So 2 and 3 are buildable today and 1 is not.
