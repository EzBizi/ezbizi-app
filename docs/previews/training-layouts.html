Training — layouts for the three tabs

Eight numbered alternatives, drawn in the app's real chrome and the pinned palette from client/lib/core/theme.dart. Answer by number: one for Learn, one for the guide player, one for Dashboard, one for Settings. Data is invented but the shape is what would ship.

Every layout obeys the house rules: no modals — a selection is a pane or a screen; no purple, no gradients; no literal colours outside the theme; and each one collapses to a single column at 375px, shown at the bottom. Scope badges (Organization A-1 Chimney Riverside) are on every card because a guide belongs to a place, and that is the fact a franchise user needs to see at a glance.

The matrix in layout 5 cannot be a matrix here. It becomes one card per person carrying the same counts — so layout 5 costs a second narrow layout that layout 6 gets free, because its list is already a list.
Answer by number
Learn — 1 or 2?

1 separates what you owe from what you may browse, and the assigned shelf can never be filtered away. 2 is denser and one control drives everything, but "assigned" becomes a chip somebody can turn off.

Player — 3 or 4?

3 is the same on every screen size and gives the content the most room. 4 shows how much is left and lets you jump back, and matches the 25/75 split Inventory and Fleet already use.

Dashboard — 5 or 6?

5 answers "who has not done this" instantly but stops fitting past roughly eight guides, and needs a separate narrow layout. 6 scales to any number and is already one column on a phone, but takes two clicks to compare two people.

Settings — 7 or 8?

7 matches every other module in the app, which is worth a lot on its own. 8 shows the guide's shape rather than a list of titles, which is better for authoring and is a layout that exists nowhere else here.

A mixed answer is fine and probably right — 1, 3, 6, 7 is a coherent set, and so is 2, 4, 5, 8. Nothing here is built yet; the pick decides what gets written.
