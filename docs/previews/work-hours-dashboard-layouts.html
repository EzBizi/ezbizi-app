With this tab▦Dashboard◷General☂Paid Time Off▤Reports⚙Settingsfirst, and what Work Hours opens on for anyone who has it
  • Four tabs, each behind its own grant. General is one person’s week at a time: people down the left, that person’s days on the right, with Auto and Outside schedule on a shift. No tab says who was late, missed a day or left early, and none adds up more than one week.
  • The numbers are already on main (GET hr/time-clock/stats, 559529c7) and nothing draws them. The route sits under General’s grant until this tab exists; its own comment says it then moves to work_hours.dashboard.view.
  • How a day is judged: its first clock-in against that person’s schedule, on their place’s clock, with the grace period (5 minutes, Work Hours › Settings); its last clock-out for leaving early, and only when they clocked out themselves. A lunch break in between is neither. Today cannot be missed, and days still to come are not counted. Outside schedule is a shift begun on a day off or more than 30 minutes before the start.
  • Who is counted: active people the reader can see who clocked in or were tagged in the period, or who have a schedule of their own. Somebody on the place’s business hours who never clocked in during the period is left out, not counted as missed.

Every option keeps

  • Only the people the reader can see in Work Hours: the same people General lists, and nobody else.
  • The period picker: This week (what it opens on), Last week, This month, Custom. Custom is two calendar days that open under their fields, at most 92 days. Days are the organization’s, and the week starts on its own week-start day.
  • On time means within the grace period from Work Hours › Settings (5 minutes here), and the screen says so beside the figures.
  • A person opens in place: a panel docked beside the list on a desk, the same area with ← back on a phone. No dialog, no new page.
  • Nothing to judge is a dash, not 0% (Wes Halloran is on vacation all week). With Clock in switched off, the tab shows the server’s sentence instead of numbers.

What it needs from the server. Sorting, search and the role filter are the client’s.

Recommendation

1 on a desk, 4 on a phone. The question was “for each employee”, and the table answers it on one screen: one row per person with every number the server already counts, the figures above it sorting it, and one person’s days beside it without leaving the list. It needs one new read, a person’s days, and one change, the usual in and out over scheduled days only. 2’s lists are what sorting 1 by Late, Missed or Forgot to clock out already shows; 3’s strip is the best picture of a month, but it needs a status for every day for everyone.

One thing to decide: somebody on the place’s business hours who never clocks in during the period is left out of every option, not counted as missed. The server does that so an owner who never clocks in does not read as missing every day. Should they count?

People, numbers and the organization are invented, and judged by the server’s own rules. Times print as the server sends them, in the organization’s 12- or 24-hour clock; hours as General prints them.
