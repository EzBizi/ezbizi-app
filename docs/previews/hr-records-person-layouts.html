HR › Records — everybody on the left, their whole record on the right, and editable

Jose, 2026-09-24: "i'd like the users to be on a left side bar and clicking on them brings out all the information, which can be edited." The desktop shape is yours already — list left, record right. These three differ in how the record is laid out and how editing works. The phone cannot hold a sidebar, so its three are designed for the phone rather than narrowed. Pick one desktop (1–3) and one phone (4–6).

What a record actually holds today — measured from the code, so nothing below is invented:
  • Personal — first, middle and last name, phone, home address, date of birth, gender, email. Today only the person can change these, in Your account. After this, HR can too — the same record, not a copy, and every change is logged with who made it.
  • Employment — role, active or deactivated, where they work, start date, and when their account was created.
  • Work — their work schedule, and whether the lock screen is switched off for them.
  • Onboarding — what they filled in and signed, per contract, plus uploaded documents.
  • Time off, Training, Access — their paid-time-off allowance and date, training, and the authenticator reset / password reset link that already live in the row's menu.

Three things stay read-only on purpose, in every option: email (it is how they sign in — changing it from here can lock them out), what onboarding collected (it is what they signed; editing it afterwards would change a signed record), and time off (it comes from the organization's policy in Settings). Start date becomes editable — nothing can record a start date that already happened today, which is why Paid time off says "we cannot tell you" for everybody who was here before it existed.

1One record, each section edits on its own recommended

Everything on one scrolling page. Each section has its own Edit, which turns just that section into fields with Save and Cancel. Fixing a phone number cannot touch their role by accident, and each save is one small, logged change.
