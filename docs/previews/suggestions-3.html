This layout draws them oldest first and scrolls to the bottom, which is the one place a stream must invert the endpoint — worth saying because it is a reverse, not a sort, and reversing a list you later paginate is where this kind of screen goes wrong. The honest problem with this idiom, stated rather than hidden It looks like you can reply, and you cannot. There is one note per suggestion and no endpoint that adds to it — the box at the bottom starts a new suggestion, always. Three things in this drawing exist to keep that from misleading anybody: the box is labelled "Send EzBizi another suggestion" rather than "Reply"; it sits under a rule with its own label instead of tucking against the last reply; and the server's own sentence — "Send another if there is more to say" — is printed under the reply it belongs to. And the rule that must be visible rather than described: Edit and Withdraw are under the new one and under nothing else, because an author may correct or take back a suggestion only while nobody has read it. In a stream that reads bottom-up this is naturally right: the newest thing is at the bottom, next to the box, and it is usually the only editable one anyway.
  • In review — "Somebody at EzBizi is reading this one, so it is fixed while they do."
  • Approved / Denied — "This one has been answered, so it stays as it was written."

3 · Your correspondence with EzBizi Everything in one stream, oldest at the top, your suggestions offset one way and EzBizi's answers offset the other, with the box docked at the bottom of the pane rather than floating at the top of the page. It makes the decision note what it actually is — a person writing back — which is the thing a denied suggestion lives or dies on, and it is the only layout where sending another after a denial is a single motion. What it trades away: it promises a conversation the backend does not have, so the wording has to work hard against the shape; and it is the worst of the three at "what happened to the one about the gate codes", because a stream has no index and nothing to filter. At forty suggestions it is a long scroll with no way in.

Desktop — the stream, all four states, scrolled to the bottom

Read it bottom to top and the presence/absence pair is the first thing you meet. Edit and Withdraw sit under the newest one, which is the New one. Above it the In review one carries a line saying it is fixed while somebody reads it, and both answered ones carry the sentence saying they stay as they were written. Nothing else offers a control. Note the text alignment: the bubbles are offset by margin, never by text-align. A 2000-character suggestion set ragged-left would be the most unreadable text in the app.

Desktop — the first visit

An empty stream is the one case this layout handles best: there is nothing to scroll past, the explanation sits in the middle of the space, and the box is already at the bottom where it will always be. Send is outlined rather than filled while there is nothing to send — outlined, not faded.

375px — designed for the phone, not narrowed

This is the phone answer the other two do not have.
