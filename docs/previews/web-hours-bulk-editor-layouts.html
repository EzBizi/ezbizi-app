Web Management · Hours for several websites at once

Asked by Web Management (server live in 0.0.0290, c77a7873), from Rodrigo's brief via Jose, 2026-09-28. One way to change the hours of any number of the company's websites, with a review that shows every site's old, requested, converted and final hours before anything is written.

Fixed by the brief and the contract, in every option below:
  • One control per day, no switch: Keep existing · Set hours · Closed all day. Under Set hours, opening and closing each say Keep or a time. A day that is closed now needs both times; a day left alone is not sent at all.
  • Websites are the destination. Google Business Profile is drawn but greyed with the server's own reason, "not switched on yet": built, not active, as Jose asked.
  • Optional: Convert hours from (a time zone; each site converts into its own), and Don't open before (applied after converting, moves only the opening).
  • The main button is Review changes, never Save. The review is the server's answer (POST /web/hours/review): per site, per day, existing → requested → converted → final, what Don't open before moved, and what is blocked and why. Update N websites applies that exact review (its token); if it is over 30 minutes old the server refuses and the screen says to review again.
  • Results per website: Changed · Unchanged · Blocked · Failed · Not confirmed (a timeout is never a success).
  • No popup menus and no dialogs: pickers are the app's search picker, anything that opens is a side panel or a page.

1One page: websites, the week, and the review docked beside it recommended

Opened from SEO › General with "Edit hours on several websites". Everything on one screen: tick the websites, the two options under them, then the week. Review changes docks the review beside the week (which locks while you read it); Update 3 websites turns the review into the results. The week stays in view the whole time, so going back to change Friday is one click.

Recommendation: 1 on a desktop, 5 on a phone. Both keep the week as seven rows, the way the current hours card already reads, so nothing has to be relearned; the review sits where the choices were made, and nothing is written from a screen that has not been reviewed. 3 is stronger for someone running a dozen sites and can be added later on the same endpoints. 6 is the quickest on a phone, but it is a different way of thinking about the week from the desktop.
