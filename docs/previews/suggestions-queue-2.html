Optimising for: the quality of the reply. The queue is a rail, one suggestion fills the pane, and the box you write the answer in is always on screen under it.

Read from the built backend (2f01245, f845c4e), not assumed GET /api/v1/staff/suggestions returns {id, organization, organization_id, author, body, status, decision_note, decided_at, created_at}. The order is the server's: undecided, then being read, then decided — and inside each band newest created_at first, so the decided band is ordered by when a thing was sent rather than when it was answered. 200 rows, no paging, no search parameter (limit(200) and nothing else), so any filtering is the client's over what already arrived, and no total may be promised. decide takes reviewing, approved or denied — never new, and a second decide on a decided one is accepted and replaces both the status and the note. That is why the footer stays on a decided row rather than turning into a read-only card. Approving or denying notifies the author; marking in review does not. The rail is 360px, and the number has a reason. It carries three facts — which customer, which person, and enough of the sentence to tell two rows from the same customer apart. The author fallback "Somebody who has since left" is 27 characters on its own, and organization names in this list are other people's business names rather than anything we control.
Three things about the decide control that came out of the code, not the drawing Denying with no note is refused, in the server's words: "Say why, in a sentence. The person who sent it reads it." A 422 on note, so it is reported at the field and retried with the same button — a refused save, never a reload. Approving with no note is legal and should not be. The notification's body is the note, so an approval with an empty note sends a bell that says "Your suggestion was approved" and nothing under it. The screen presses for a sentence even though the server does not. Marking one "In review" must not offer the note field. decide writes any note it is given, and the author's payload carries decision_note whatever the status — so a note typed beside "In review" turns up on the author's screen with no notification and no explanation. "Mark in review" is therefore a bare button in all three layouts.
One thing worth your ruling This is the seventh chip in the Development tab strip, and that strip scrolls sideways. TabBarLike is a horizontally scrolling row of chips, against the house rule that chrome wraps rather than scrolls. It is a SingleChildScrollView(Axis.horizontal), so it scrolls by construction rather than only when it happens to be too wide, and the six labels already there do not come near fitting 375px. A seventh makes it worse rather than causing it. The fix is one line, but TabBarLike is shared with Automations, so it changes two screens. It is drawn wrapped here. Say the word and it goes in with the build.
2 · List beside the answer The app's own dominant idiom — a rail of rows and a pane that reads the selected one — with the answer box living in the pane's footer rather than appearing when you ask for it. The suggestion is on screen in full while you write the reply to it, which is the whole argument for this one: these replies are read word for word by the person who sent them, and the difference between a good denial and a bad one is whether the writer still had the request in front of them. What it trades away: the rail shows about half the rows layout 1 shows, so the shape of the whole queue — how many, from how many customers — is never visible at once; and it is the slowest of the three when the right answer to six of the nine is obvious from the first line.

Desktop — a new one selected, the answer box in the footer

The rail carries the bands the server sorted into, the organization and the author on every row. Mark in review is a bare button beside the box and says what it does and does not do: it stops the author rewriting it, and tells them nothing.

Desktop — Deny pressed with an empty note, and refused

The server's sentence at the field, the field outlined in the error colour, the button unchanged. Nothing here offers a reload — a refused save is retried by pressing the same button, and a retry that reloaded would recover the note by throwing it away.

Desktop — an answered one, and the reversal the API allows

A decided row is not read-only. decide accepts a second decision and replaces the status and the note, so the footer stays, pre-filled with what was said, and the buttons read as a change rather than a first answer. The existing answer is also shown in the pane above, where the author sees it, so nobody rewrites a note without seeing the one they are replacing.

375px — designed for the phone, not narrowed

There is no split at 375px, so the rail is the screen and the pane is a push. The answer screen keeps the suggestion above the box — the one thing this layout exists to preserve — and the buttons dock above the keyboard.
