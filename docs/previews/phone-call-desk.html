Phone — the Call desk, three ways

Same palette as client/lib/core/theme.dart, in the app's own chrome, with A-1 Chimney's real lines. Answer by number.

What is wrong with the one that shipped

  • The keypad is the whole screen. Twelve stadium-shaped buttons roughly 400 × 130px each. A dial pad is a small, dense thing — every phone ever made agrees on that.
  • It is the loudest element, and the least used. Most calls are answered, not dialled; and most dialling is a customer you click, not digits you type.
  • Nothing says which line you are on. A-1 Chimney has five. Calling out from the wrong one shows the customer the wrong number.
  • The customer column is empty until you type, so two-thirds of the screen is blank at rest.
  • Teammates is a heading with one name under it, taking a full column.

1 · Recents first, keypad on demand (recommended)

The desk opens on what actually happens — the last calls, ready to call back in one click. The keypad is a small panel you open when you genuinely need to type a number. Teammates and the customer share the right column, so nothing is blank at rest.

Why 1

  • It opens on the work. Recents with a call-back button is what a field-service desk does all day; a dial pad is the exception, not the default.
  • Nothing is blank at rest — the current desk shows an empty customer column until you type.
  • "Calling from" is on screen permanently, which is the bug that costs money: five lines, and dialling from the wrong one shows the customer a number that is not yours.
  • 2 is the safer pick if you want the pad always visible — it is the current layout with the proportions fixed rather than a rethink.
  • 3 reads best on a phone and worst on a monitor: a dispatcher cannot see who is free without opening a strip.
