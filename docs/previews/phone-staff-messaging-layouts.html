Phone › Call — staff messaging

The ask, as Phone briefed it Colleagues messaging each other inside EzBizi. Not SMS to mobiles, not customer messaging — person to person, staff only. It lives on the Team panel of Phone › Call, which already lists colleagues with a presence dot and lets you ring one: “call this person” exists, “message this person” does not. The user is at a desk mid-shift wanting to ask a colleague something without ringing them — often while on another call, which is the case that makes it worth building. Data is sender, recipient, body, sent time, read time. Backend is general staff messaging, not Phone-specific, so a second surface later is not trapped inside Phone.
The constraint that shapes every layout: there is no realtime transport No websocket, no push. Delivery is polling. So “sent” and “seen” are the only honest states — there is no delivered, no typing indicator, no live presence on a message. Every layout here says when it last checked, in words, at the bottom of the thread. A chat UI that looks like the ones people use all day is writing a cheque the transport cannot cash: it will feel broken at 20 seconds of lag, not because 20 seconds is bad but because the design promised instant. This is the same shape as two things already approved: Trust says “not captured” rather than drawing a 0% bar, and Scheduling says “configured capacity” rather than “remaining”. Each time the fix was to name what the number actually is instead of implying the thing the system cannot do.
Two things I need you to rule on 1 · Unread has to escape the panel, and I have put it in three places. Phone's own constraint: “a message sent to somebody looking at Records is a message they never see”. So every layout badges the sidebar Phone item, the Call tab, and the Team column header. That is deliberate over-signalling for a first version — if it proves noisy, the sidebar badge is the one to drop, because it is the only one visible from outside the module. 2 · Layout 3 puts staff threads in the same column as customer SMS, and I recommend against it. It is the tidiest use of space and the column is already “the conversation column”. But one surface for two audiences means a reply can go to the wrong one, and the wrong one is a customer receiving an internal note. I have drawn it with a hard segmented switch and a persistent colour difference, so you can judge whether that is enough. My view is that it is not, at any width, and that the risk is asymmetric.

Pick a number. 1 is my recommendation. 4 is it at 375px.
