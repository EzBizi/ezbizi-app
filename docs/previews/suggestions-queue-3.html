Development › Suggestions — layout 3 · Everything, in full, in one column

Optimising for: reading rather than triaging. Nothing is truncated, nothing has to be opened, and the box you answer in is already on every card.

Read from the built backend (2f01245, f845c4e), not assumed GET /api/v1/staff/suggestions returns {id, organization, organization_id, author, body, status, decision_note, decided_at, created_at}. The order is the server's: undecided, then being read, then decided — and inside each band newest created_at first, so the decided band is ordered by when a thing was sent, not when it was answered. 200 rows, no paging, no search parameter. That cap matters more to this layout than to the other two, and it is the one number that decides whether this layout is sane: at full-height cards, 200 rows is a page nobody reaches the bottom of. decide takes reviewing, approved or denied — never new, and a second decide on a decided one replaces the status and the note, which is why the answered cards keep a live footer instead of turning into receipts. Approving or denying notifies the author; marking in review does not. The author fallback is "Somebody who has since left" and the organization fallback is "Unknown". Both are drawn below.
Three things about the decide control that came out of the code, not the drawing Denying with no note is refused, in the server's words: "Say why, in a sentence. The person who sent it reads it." A 422 on note, reported at the field and retried with the same button — a refused save, never a reload. Approving with no note is legal and should not be. The notification's body is the note, so an approval with an empty note sends a bell reading "Your suggestion was approved" with nothing under it. Marking one "In review" must not offer the note field. The author's payload carries decision_note whatever the status, so a note typed beside "In review" appears on their screen with no notification and no explanation. "Mark in review" is a bare button in all three layouts. And a risk that belongs to this layout alone: nine open note boxes on one page is nine places to type into the wrong one. The card being written in therefore takes the focus ring and the rest stay plainly bordered — a ring rather than a fade, because fading the cards you are not using would fade the text you are reading them for.
One thing worth your ruling This is the seventh chip in the Development tab strip, and that strip scrolls sideways. TabBarLike is a horizontally scrolling row of chips, against the house rule that chrome wraps rather than scrolls. It is a SingleChildScrollView(Axis.horizontal), so it scrolls by construction rather than only when it happens to be too wide, and the six labels already there do not come near fitting 375px. A seventh makes it worse rather than causing it. The fix is one line, but TabBarLike is shared with Automations, so it changes two screens. It is drawn wrapped here.
3 · Everything, in full, in one column No table, no rail, no expanding: one column of cards in the server's order, each showing the whole suggestion and carrying its own answer box. It is the only one of the three where every word every customer wrote is on the screen at once, which is what you want when the job is not "clear the queue" but "find out what people are actually asking for" — three customers describing the same missing feature in three different vocabularies is invisible in a truncated table and obvious here. What it trades away: it does not scale. Nine cards is a long page and two hundred is not a page at all, and there is no shape to the queue — you cannot see how many are waiting without scrolling and counting. It is also the most vertical space spent on suggestions that have already been answered.

Desktop — the feed, every body in full, an answer box on every card

The bands are the server's order made visible, not a client-side sort. Every card carries the organization and the author because this list spans every customer. The answered cards keep a live footer reading "Change the answer", because decide accepts a second decision, and the answer the author was given is quoted above it so nobody rewrites a note without seeing the one they are replacing.

Desktop — Deny pressed with an empty note, and refused

The server's sentence at the field, the field outlined in the error colour, the button unchanged so the retry is the same press. No reload offered anywhere near it — this is a refused save, and a reload would recover the note by discarding it.

375px — designed for the phone, not narrowed

The feed is already one column, so narrowing it would have been the lazy answer and it is not this one. A phone screen holds one card, so the phone version deals them one at a time: the whole suggestion fills the screen, the position in the queue is in the bar, and Approve / Deny / Mark in review dock at the thumb. That keeps this layout's actual claim — you read the whole thing before you answer — which a scrolling feed of truncated phone cards would quietly drop.
